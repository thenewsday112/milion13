Tempeh adds nutty flavor and 9 grams of protein to this Thai-inspired takeout favorite (1). Be sure to read labels when buying fish sauce as some brands contain a lot more sodium than others. 

Active time: 25 minutes | Total time: 30 minutes

Tempeh Pad Thai With Cabbage and Corn

Ingredients

  • 6 oz (170g) flat, medium-width dry brown rice noodles (often described on package as “Pad Thai noodles”) 
  • 1 tbsp brown sugar
  • 1 tbsp fish sauce
  • 1 tbsp sriracha hot sauce
  • 1 tbsp tamarind concentrate
  • 1 1/2 tbsp coconut or safflower oil
  • 6 oz (170g) tempeh, cut into bite-size strips
  • 2 large garlic cloves, sliced
  • 2 cups (180g) chopped green cabbage
  • 1/2 medium red bell pepper, thinly sliced
  • 1/2 cup (73g) fresh corn (shaved off 1 cob)
  • 2 large eggs, beaten
  • 3 green onions, thinly sliced
  • 1/4 cup chopped cilantro
  • 1 lime, cut into quarters

Directions

Prepare the noodles according to package instructions, erring on the side of slightly underdone (the noodles will continue to cook when stir-fried). Drain, rinse with cool water and set aside.

In a small bowl, combine 2 tbsp hot water with the brown sugar, fish sauce, sriracha and tamarind. Stir and set aside.

Heat the oil over medium-high heat in a wok or large, nonstick sauté pan. Add the tempeh and stir-fry until browned in places, 2 minutes. Add the garlic and stir-fry until fragrant, 20 seconds. Add the cabbage, bell pepper and corn and stir-fry until the cabbage is wilted and tender, 2 minutes.

Push the tempeh and vegetables to the side of the pan. Add the eggs and stir-fry until just cooked, 1 minute. Add the noodles and sauce mixture to the wok and stir-fry everything together until the noodles sear in places, 1–2 minutes. Add the green onions and toss to combine. Divide the noodles among 4 bowls and sprinkle with the cilantro. Serve with lime wedges on the side.

Serves: 4 | Serving Size: 1 1/2 cups

Nutrition (per serving): Calories: 369; Total Fat: 12g; Saturated Fat: 2g; Monounsaturated Fat: 6g; Polyunsaturated Fat: 3g ; Cholesterol: 103mg; Sodium: 339mg; Carbohydrate: 61g; Dietary Fiber: 6g; Sugar: 8g; Protein: 16g

Nutrition Bonus: Potassium: 406mg; Iron: 16%; Calcium: 9%; Vitamin A: 17%; Vitamin C: 74%

Traditionally made with rice, congee is a comfort food staple in Chinese cuisine, but this lighter take on the classic porridge swaps rice for oats. With the addition of spices, veggies and lean protein with chicken, you have a delicious meal in 15 minutes for about 400 calories.

Active time: 15 minutes | Total time: 15 minutes

Chicken Congee

Ingredients

  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 3 cloves garlic, chopped fine
  • 2 shallots, chopped fine
  • 3.5oz (100g) boneless, skinless cooked chicken breast, minced 
  • 1/2 tsp low-sodium soy sauce 
  • 2 cups (500g) water
  • 1/2 tsp low-sodium chicken bouillon powder
  • 1 cup (100g) quick-cooking oats
  • 1 tsp sesame oil
  • 3 celery stalks, thinly sliced on the diagonal
  • Ground black pepper, to taste

Directions

Heat the olive oil in a pan and add the chopped garlic and shallot to the pan. Sauté until both are browned. Add the minced chicken and soy sauce. Mix well and set aside.

Cook the oats with the water and chicken bouillon powder until thickened to your liking. Add the seasoned chicken to the porridge and stir well. 

Divide into 2 bowls and garnish with the celery, sesame oil and black pepper to taste. Serve hot.

Serves: 2 | Serving Size: 1/2 of recipe

Nutrition (per serving): Calories: 419; Total Fat: 22g; Saturated Fat: 4g; Monounsaturated Fat: 13g; Polyunsaturated Fat: 5g; Cholesterol: 43mg; Sodium: 419mg; Carbohydrate: 37g; Dietary Fiber: 7g; Sugar: 4g; Protein: 21g

Nutrition Bonus: Potassium: 663mg; Iron: 16%; Vitamin C: 7%; Calcium: 7%

Mexican Burrito with Chicken & Avocado

📅 Oct 20, 2026  |  Category: Food  |  Read Source →

Why buy burritos when they’re so simple to make? Save time with precooked chicken (you can also use leftovers!) and budget-friendly staples like canned beans, shredded cheese, salsa and frozen corn. Then, step up your burrito game by adding creamy avocado and fresh lime juice. Wrap in foil, and pack for a homemade lunch.

Active time: 15 minutes | Total time: 20 minutes

Mexican Burrito with Chicken & Avocado

Ingredients

  • 8 oz (227g) boneless, skinless chicken breasts, cooked 
  • 1/4 cup (38g) fresh or frozen corn (thawed)
  • 1/4 cup (43g) no salt added black beans, drained and rinsed
  • 2 tbsp finely chopped cilantro, divided (plus more for optional garnish)
  • 1/2 cup (56g) shredded reduced-sodium Cheddar cheese
  • 1 small avocado (150g), diced and divided
  • 1/4 cup (60g) low-sodium tomatillo salsa, divided (plus more for optional garnish)
  • 1 tsp fresh lime juice
  • 1/4 tsp salt (or to taste)
  • 1/4 tsp pepper (or to taste)
  • 3 medium (8-inch/45g each) whole-wheat flour tortillas
  • Diced tomatoes (optional garnish)

Directions

Warm the chicken (if desired) and chop into bite-size pieces.

In a small bowl, combine the chicken, corn, black beans, 1 tbsp cilantro, cheese, 1/2 avocado and 2 tbsp salsa. Mix well to combine. Add salt and pepper. In a blender or food processor, combine the lime juice and the remaining avocado, salsa and cilantro. Blend until creamy. Taste for seasoning, adding more lime juice or salt as needed.

Spread 1/3 of the chicken filling into the center of a flour tortilla. Fold in the sides, and bring up the bottom of the tortilla, tucking in the ends. Keep rolling, pressing and creasing the end of the tortilla as you finish rolling it up. Top with avocado cream, and, if desired, more salsa, cilantro and tomatoes.

Serves: 3 | Serving Size: 1 burrito

Nutrition (per serving): Calories: 456; Total Fat: 20g; Saturated Fat: 7g; Monounsaturated Fat: 8g; Polyunsaturated Fat: 3g; Cholesterol: 83mg; Sodium: 566mg; Carbohydrate: 34g; Dietary Fiber: 8g; Sugar: 4g; Protein: 35g

Nutrition Bonus: Potassium: 535mg; Iron: 20%; Vitamin A: 10%; Vitamin C: 25%; Calcium: 25%

Advice for GLP-1 Users

📅 Oct 02, 2026  |  Category: Food  |  Read Source →

Someone asked me what I’d recommend to someone taking GLP drugs. I’ve written posts and newsletters on this before, but there’s one newer bit of advice I haven’t mentioned before: eat more.

The thing with these drugs is that they work incredibly well. Whether it’s semaglutide (Ozempic), tirzepatide (Mounjaro), or the yet-to-be-FDA-approved retatrutide, these compounds are very good at getting you to eat less. They have other effects, too, like slowed gastric emptying, “slower” digestion, and others—some positive, some more negative.

But the one thing almost everyone reports is that you eat far less food on these things.

Subscribe now

That can be good if you need it. Most people eat too much. Even if it’s healthy food. I remember being at PrimalCon back in the day and being shocked by the amount of food people could put away. Everything was high quality, cooked in good fats, well-sourced, but people would routinely report that they gained weight at PrimalCon. Food is food. Volume is volume. And that’s why these drugs are having such powerful effects in the overweight and obese. They’re losing weight because they aren’t eating very much and, most importantly, they aren’t hungry doing it.

But what about when you start losing too much? When you start losing muscle, losing energy?

Everyone says the obvious: “make your calories count.” Get your micronutrients. Make it high quality, whole foods. With less volume coming in, the quality of what little volume you do get becomes increasingly important.

And they talk about protein, too, rightfully. A big part of countering the negative effects of GLP drugs, like muscle loss, requires that you stay active. Lift weights, and support that activity by eating protein.

These are all good, but you’ve heard them before.

The one piece of advice you might not have heard, that sounds a bit unconventional, but that becomes useful especially as you start approaching your ideal weight: eat more.

Try to eat a bit more than you feel like.

Many issues we see with these drugs are from undernourishment. You’re running a deficit and, depending on the drug in question, burning more energy. You need to eat a little more than you think.

It’ll still be “less,” don’t worry.
