Chilled buckwheat soba noodles are traditionally served as a cooling meal in the heat of summer in Japan. In this salad, the noodles are tossed with plenty of veggies, grilled chicken and a gingery dressing. 

RD Tip: Be sure to buy noodles with a high percentage of whole-grain buckwheat flour for the most beneficial fiber.

Active time: 35 minutes | Total time: 45 minutes

Soba Noodle Salad With Ginger Dressing

Ingredients

  • 2 tbsp low-sodium soy sauce
  • 2 tbsp rice vinegar
  • 1 tbsp honey
  • 2 tsp toasted sesame oil
  • 3 tsp ginger, finely grated
  • 3 medium garlic cloves, finely chopped
  • 1 tbsp canola oil
  • 3 cups (85g) shiitake mushrooms, stems discarded, caps sliced
  • 1/4 tsp salt
  • 1/4 tsp ground black pepper
  • 2 (8 oz/227g each) boneless, skinless chicken breasts
  • 4 cups (227g) broccoli crowns, cut into 2-inch florets
  • 1/2 cup (75g) shelled edamame
  • 6 oz (170g) dry buckwheat soba noodles 
  • 3 green onions, thinly sliced

Directions

In a small bowl, combine the soy sauce, vinegar, honey, sesame oil, ginger and garlic. Set aside.

Heat the canola oil in a cast-iron skillet or heavy sauté pan over medium-high heat. Add the mushrooms and sauté until tender, 4 minutes. Transfer to a large serving bowl. Season the chicken with the salt and pepper before adding it to the skillet, reduce the heat to medium, and cook until cooked through and an instant-read thermometer registers 165°F (74°C), about 5 minutes per side. Place on a cutting board and let rest, uncovered, for 10 minutes.

While the chicken rests, bring a medium saucepan of water to a boil. Add the broccoli and edamame and cook until the broccoli is crisp-tender, 4 minutes. With a slotted spoon, transfer the vegetables to the serving bowl with the mushrooms. Add the soba noodles to the boiling water and cook according to package instructions. Drain and rinse with cool water to stop the noodles from overcooking. Add the noodles to the vegetables in the bowl. Toss with the soy sauce dressing.

Thinly slice the chicken against the grain and place on top of the noodle mixture. Sprinkle green onions over the top and serve. (The salad can be stored in an airtight container for up to 2 days.)

Serves: 4 | Serving Size: 2 cups noodle mixture plus 4 oz chicken

Nutrition (per serving): Calories: 357; Total Fat: 10g; Saturated Fat: 1g; Monounsaturated Fat: 4g; Polyunsaturated Fat: 3g; Cholesterol: 51mg; Sodium: 584mg; Carbohydrate: 43g; Dietary Fiber: 6g; Sugar: 8g; Protein: 29g

Nutrition Bonus: Potassium: 703mg; Iron: 15%; Vitamin A: 46%; Vitamin C: 117%; Calcium: 9%

Britain’s Niall Darwen won the 2026 Classic Physique Olympia, ahead of Germany’s Mike Sommerfeld and 2025 champion Ramon Dino. It is the second straight year the division has a new champion since Chris Bumstead’s run of six titles ended in 2024, and it capped a remarkable weekend for British athletes: Ryan Terry won Men’s Physique and Samson Dauda was runner-up in the Men’s Open.

Division by division

212: Keone Pearson beat Brazil’s Lucas Garcia and former champion Shaun Clarida. Pearson’s structure — a small waist and full muscle bellies — is exactly what 212 judges reward.

Ms. Olympia: Andrea Shaw won again, with Ashley Lynnette Jones second. Our sources disagree on which of the two won in 2024 and 2025, so our Ms. Olympia history marks those years as being verified.

Fitness: Michelle Fredua-Mensah completed an Arnold Classic–Olympia double, the only athlete to win the same division at both shows in 2026.

Wellness: Brazil’s Eduarda Bezerra won the division that rewards lower-body development, where Brazilian athletes have been dominant.

Our take

The story of the weekend was Britain. Darwen, Terry and Dauda give the UK its best Olympia result in memory, and Darwen’s win suggests Classic Physique’s post-Bumstead era will be decided by balance and flow rather than raw size.

Pearson is the 212 champion with the best chance of a long reign; Shaw remains the benchmark in women’s bodybuilding.

Full Men’s Open placings are in our Mr. Olympia report, and the complete Olympia results are on our Olympia Weekend results page.

The British breakthrough

British bodybuilding has produced Olympia champions before — Dorian Yates won six straight Mr. Olympia titles from 1992 to 1997 and Samson Dauda won in 2024 — but never on this scale in a single weekend. Darwen’s Classic Physique win, Terry’s Men’s Physique win and Dauda’s Open runner-up spot reflect a deep pool of UK talent and coaching.

Nick Walker is Mr. Olympia. The American won the Men’s Open title at Olympia Weekend in Las Vegas (September 24–27, 2026), finishing two points ahead of 2024 champion Samson Dauda and six ahead of defending champion Derek Lunsford, who placed third.

The win was worth $600,000 and completed a long climb for Walker, who won the Arnold Classic in 2021 and has been one of the sport’s most popular competitors since. Lee Haney and Ronnie Coleman still share the record of eight titles.

Bodybuilding is scored by placings: each judge ranks the competitors and the lowest total wins. A two-point gap between first and second means the panel was close to split between Walker and Dauda, while the 16-point gap between Lunsford in third and Andrew Jacked in fourth shows how clearly the top three separated themselves.

How Walker beat the Arnold champion

Andrew Jacked beat Walker at the Arnold Classic in March and won the $750,000 purse. Six months later the order flipped decisively: Walker won, and Andrew Jacked finished fourth. It is a familiar pattern — the March winner peaks for Columbus and does not quite reach the same level in September — and it made Walker’s win more convincing.

Our take

This was the right result. Walker brought the most complete package of size, density and conditioning, and he improved on the physique that finished behind Andrew Jacked in March. Dauda’s narrow second keeps him firmly in the conversation for 2027; Lunsford’s third shows how quickly the Open is moving.

The Men’s Open has had five different winners in the last six Olympias — Big Ramy, Hadi Choopan, Derek Lunsford, Samson Dauda, Lunsford again and now Walker. That is good for the sport’s storylines but makes long reigns, like Haney’s and Coleman’s, look further away than ever.

Brandon Curry, Mr. Olympia in 2019, finishing ninth is the clearest sign that the division has turned over.

How the Olympia is judged

The Men’s Open is judged across symmetry, mandatory poses — front double biceps, front lat spread, side chest, back double biceps, back lat spread, side triceps, abs and thigh, and most muscular — comparisons between the top contenders, and a final posing routine. Judges rank each competitor; the rankings are added up and the lowest total wins. Ties are broken by the placings in specific rounds.

Is Keto Dead?

📅 Sep 14, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Yes and no.

Keto dieting’s popularity rose and peaked in the late 20-teens, declined for a few years, and has more or less held steady since 2023. So says Google Trends anyway. During the keto heyday, it felt like every time I went to the grocery store there were a dozen new products proclaiming “KETO” on the label. Most of the reader questions sent my way had to do with troubleshooting their keto diets. For a while, it was the topic I covered most frequently on the blog—what to eat and avoid, how to supplement, how to make it work for specific populations.

Not so much anymore.

Subscribe now

But don’t be fooled. Although keto is no longer front and center in most diet and weight loss conversations—that distinction goes to GLP-1s—people are still discovering keto diets and trying them for themselves. Plenty of folks, including friends of mine, have been keto for many years, some even predating the keto boom. New books are still being written. Bloggers are still putting out keto recipe guides.

Keto is here to stay. It just looks a little different, as I say in the video.

Remember, the liver starts producing ketones when insulin is low and liver glycogen becomes depleted. Many roads lead to this outcome. Any way of eating that restricts daily carb intake below about 50 grams per day will trigger ketosis. Intermittent fasting paired with a Primal-style diet could do it. Carnivore diets can do it. Even a moderately low-carb diet paired with long or intense training sessions can do it. I’d wager that a fair number of you reading this are in ketosis at least some of the time, whether or not you’re intentionally following a keto diet. I’m sure I am too, although I don’t measure my ketone levels.

I still believe that keto is one of the best tools any of us have in our metabolic toolboxes for losing weight, tackling insulin resistance, and building that all-important metabolic flexibility.

I don’t really care how someone chooses to label their way of eating as long as it works for them.

In some ways, it is good that keto flies more under the radar now. At the height of its popularity, keto attracted a lot of negative press. (Paleo got the same treatment when it became popular 20 years ago. People love to bash anything that flouts conventional wisdom and asks them to give up their morning donuts and 500 calorie latte treats.) Now folks are free to try it without hearing every day about how it keto is a dangerous fad diet (it’s not) that will make their heart or kidneys explode (it won’t).

Ever have those days where your digestion feels off and you just feel… blah?

Not sick, not super bloated...just kind of puffy, sluggish, not yourself?

I’ve totally been there, and I’ve found that how I eat on those days can make such a difference.

Here’s what I usually eat in a day when I’m trying to reset and feel better:

Morning:
I start my day with my hormone-balancing coffee blend (I always add Miss Congeniality Mood Boost - it’s a staple in my routine). I keep breakfast super simple: a protein smoothie or eggs + sourdough with avocado. Nothing too heavy.

Lunch:
I go for easy-to-digest foods, usually something like ground turkey with jasmine rice and roasted zucchini. Warm, nourishing, and gentle on my stomach.

Afternoon:
I always take my Bloat Be Gone before and after meals on days like this, the digestive enzymes and herbs help reduce puffiness and support smoother digestion when things feel off. It’s been a game-changer for me.

Dinner:
Something like grilled salmon with roasted sweet potatoes and sautéed greens. I aim for warm, cooked veggies instead of raw, it’s much easier on digestion.

Bonus tip:
I skip sparkling water, gum, and raw cruciferous veggies (like broccoli or kale salads) when I feel off. They always make it worse.

If you ever feel like your digestion is off, try giving your gut a little extra love that day. It doesn’t have to be complicated, just intentional. 

Hope this helps you feel more in tune with your body.
